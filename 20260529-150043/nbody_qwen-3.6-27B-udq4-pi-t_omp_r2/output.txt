### `nbody/nbody.cpp`
Three functions were parallelized:

1. **`computeForces`** (the O(n²) hotspot):
   - `#pragma omp parallel for schedule(static)` on the outer loop — each body's force is independent
   - `#pragma omp simd reduction(+:Fx,Fy,Fz)` on the inner loop — enables vectorization of the force accumulation
   - Body `i`'s position is hoisted into local scalars (`pi`, `py`, `pz`) to improve cache/SIMD behavior

2. **`integrateBodies`**:
   - `#pragma omp parallel for schedule(static)` — each body integrates independently

3. **`computeTotalEnergy`** (called during validation):
   - Kinetic energy: `#pragma omp parallel for reduction(+:energy) schedule(static)`
   - Potential energy: `#pragma omp parallel for reduction(+:energy) schedule(static)` — each outer-loop iteration contributes independently to the sum

**Correctness verified**: Identical output hashes across 1/2/4/8 threads. **Near-linear speedup**: 153ms→22ms (1→8 threads) with 2048 bodies.
